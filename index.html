Trading bot · autonomous · paper

A model judges the setup. Hard rules decide the order.

Every 30 seconds it scans the stocks in play and sends each setup to Claude, which takes it, passes or reduces size. A risk gate between the model and the broker approves, resizes or rejects every order.

  • Bracket orders, trailing stops, end-of-day exits
  • Circuit breakers for daily loss and losing streaks
  • Counterfactual replay of every signal, taken or not
  • Paper only, until an edge is proven

02 / about

From buying ads to building what runs them.

I started in paid media in 2018 and spent the years after on direct-response acquisition: fintech, financial publishing, insurance, home services and e-commerce.

Over time the work became less about the ad and more about the system under it: tracking, source tagging, retargeting, and then scripts that watched campaigns so I didn't have to. 03 / principles

The rules every system here follows.

  1. 01

    Agents draft. People decide.

    Nothing that spends, sends or publishes goes out without a person. In Quorum that's every ad, audience and budget change.

  2. 02

    One job per agent.

    A narrow job gives an output you can check. One large prompt doing everything is hard to review and hard to fix.

  3. 03

    Only what's true.

    Every claim in an ad comes from the client's website or the client. Reviews are quoted word for word.

  4. 04

    Leave receipts.

    Every request logs its time, model and output, so any answer can be traced. See the growth ops agent.

  5. 05

    Paper before money.

    The trading bot proves or disproves an edge on paper before any real money is involved.
